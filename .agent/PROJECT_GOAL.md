# Project Goal

## Goal

Create a persistent digital companion that gives the user the sustained experience of raising and living with an individual pet monster. It must not be a chatbot wearing an animated skin. It has its own needs, desires, preferences, motivations, goals, habits, emotional condition, memories, and self-selected behavior. It visually appears continuously alive, learns through interaction, develops over lived experience, and preserves one identity across sessions, sleep, restart, and long absence.

A separate caregiving core operates beneath the companion experience. It learns the user's routines and detects evidence that something materially abnormal or dangerous may be happening. It must verify uncertainty where possible and use a dedicated trusted-contact escalation process when assistance may be needed, particularly for users with special support needs.

## Objective success criteria

1. The same individual creature retains identity, temperament, relationships, memories, learned skills, and unfinished goals over meaningful time and across restart.
2. The creature spends ordinary time doing coherent things for its own reasons rather than idling until prompted.
3. It distinguishes its person, attends to faces and gestures, follows demonstrations, and responds appropriately to real multimodal interaction.
4. Repeated lived teaching changes later behavior, language, skill, expectations, and reasoning observably and within defined bounds.
5. Meaningful experiences persist while redundant detail is compressed or forgotten, and corrections supersede false learning without silently rewriting history.
6. Animation, attention, timing, state transitions, and environmental grounding read as one continuously alive creature rather than disconnected clips.
7. The separate caregiving core correctly detects, verifies, and escalates explicitly qualified emergency and abnormal-routine scenarios while controlling false alarms and preserving an audit trail.
8. Loss of network, model, microphone, camera, or another optional capability degrades awareness honestly without corrupting identity or organism state.
9. The complete system is reproducible, recoverable, supportable, privacy-preserving, appropriately licensed, and demonstrated at the integrated target-environment layer.

## Non-goals

- A chatbot or language model presented as the creature's identity, memory authority, organism state, or safety authority.
- Claims of consciousness.
- Medical diagnosis or representation as a guaranteed substitute for emergency services.
- Automatic inheritance of architecture, code, assets, models, schemas, tests, terminology, or decisions from prior companion, lifeform, monitoring, or assistant projects.
- Success claims based only on isolated demos, mocked integrations, scripted scenarios, generated narrative, or self-reported implementation summaries.

## Hard constraints

- The embodied creature and caregiving/safety core remain architecturally distinct.
- The creature may influence the presentation of a check-in, but it may not suppress, fabricate, or independently authorize a safety escalation.
- Canonical identity, organism state, memory, consent, contacts, and safety policy must not be owned by an unconstrained language model or rendering engine.
- Perception and inferred emotion remain uncertain observations with provenance, confidence, and temporal context; no single weak signal becomes canonical truth or a sole safety trigger.
- The safety core uses deterministic policy, staged verification, explicit escalation, visible failure, and immutable audit evidence.
- Core identity, memory, consent, basic interaction, safety policy, backup, export, and restore require a local survival path.
- Privacy, access control, encryption, revocation, data minimization, and conservative biometric handling are design requirements.
- Code, model weights, datasets, voices, assets, and services require independent rights, license, provenance, and risk review.
- Negative, partial, blocked, unavailable, and failed results remain recorded and may not be promoted into stronger claims.
- Meaningful learning and memory changes require evidence; generated dreams or reflections cannot silently become factual experience.

## Authoritative sources and conflict rules

- The operator-approved Notion page and linked canonical hierarchy are normative for project meaning, desired outcomes, requirements, decisions, status, and interpretation of evidence.
- GitHub is authoritative for committed code, versioned repository artifacts, branches, diffs, and exact revisions.
- Exact-head CI, tests, runtime observations, and target-environment artifacts are authoritative for demonstrated behavior at their actual evidence layer.
- `.agent/` is the repository-resident synchronized state and append-only history for Codex; it must be reconciled with Notion and GitHub rather than silently overriding either.
- Current executable evidence outranks stale implementation summaries. Current owner rulings outrank earlier product assumptions. Conflicts must be recorded and resolved explicitly.

## Acceptance authority and evidence discipline

- Codex may report technical evidence and a provisional result, but may not assign Architect, owner, or independent acceptance to its own work.
- Project stages advance only after the authorized Architect or operator reviews the required evidence.
- Use the Authority evidence ladder and classify important claims as `VERIFIED`, `PROVISIONAL`, `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS`, `INFERRED`, `UNKNOWN`, or `DISPROVEN`.
- A commit or new target test alone does not prove integrated or operational success.

## Goal-change rule

This file is the durable project constitution. Normal progress must not require rewriting it.

Change it only when the owner changes the desired finished product, immutable principles, governance or acceptance authority, or the definition of project completion. Current phases, directives, bottlenecks, dependencies, versions, architecture hypotheses, and repository status belong in the living project state, not here.
